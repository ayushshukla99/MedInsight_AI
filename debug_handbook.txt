1. DIABETES MELLITUS (DM) 
Definition 
A metabolic disorder characterized by chronic hyperglycemia resulting from defects in insulin 
secretion, insulin action, or both, leading to disturbances in carbohydrate, fat, and protein 
metabolism. 
Etiology 
 Type 1DM: Autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta-cells (Type IV hypersensitivity), leading to 
absolute insulin deficiency. Associated with HLA-DR3 and HLA-DR4. 
 Type 2 DM: Progressive insulin secretory defect on the background of insulin resistance. 
Associated with obesity, genetics (polygenic), and sedentary lifestyle. 
 Other Specific Types: Genetic defects of beta-cell function (MODY), pancreatic exocrine disease 
(chronic pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis), endocrinopathies (Cushing's, Acromegaly), drug-induced 
(Corticosteroids, Thiazides). 
 Gestational DM: Onset or first recognition during pregnancy. 
Pathophysiology 
 Type 1: Genetic predisposition + environmental trigger (e.g., Coxsackie B virus) - 
Autoantibodies (Anti-GAD65, ICA, IAA) - beta-cell destruction - Loss of insulin - Uninhibited 
gluconeogenesis/glycogenolysis and lipolysis - Hyperglycemia and Ketoacidosis. 
 Type 2: Visceral obesity/adipokines cause peripheral insulin resistance (decreased GLUT-4 
translocation in muscle/fat) - Compensatory hyperinsulinemia - Eventual beta-cell exhaustion 
and apoptosis (amylin amyloid deposition) - Hyperglycemia. Incretin effect (GLP-1/GIP) is also 
blunted. 
Clinical Features 
 Classic Triad: Polyuria (osmotic diuresis), Polydipsia (thirst from hyperosmolarity), Polyphagia. 
 Other: Unexplained weight loss (Type 1), blurred vision, fatigue, delayed wound healing, recurrent 
infections (candidiasis, UTI). 
 Acanthosis Nigricans: Velvety hyperpigmentation in skin folds (marker of insulin resistance). 
Diagnosis & Investigations 
 1120
2-hour Post-Prandial (75g OGTT): > 200 mg/dL. 
 HbAlc: > 6.5% (must be standardized to DCCT assay). 
 Random Plasma Glucose: > 200 mg/dL with classic symptoms of hyperglycemia or crisis. 
 Note: In the absence of unequivocal hyperglycemia, results require two abnormal test results 
from the same sample or in two separate test samples. 
 Investigations: Lipid profile, Serum creatinine, eGFR, Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR), 
Fundoscopy, ECG. Autoantibodies (Type 1), C-peptide (low in Type 1, high/normal in Type 2). 
Treatment 
 Type 1DM: Lifelong Insulin therapy (Basal-Bolus regimen is physiological). 
 Type2DM: 
 Lifestyle: Diet modification, exercise (150 min/week), weight loss. 
o First-line: Metformin (decreases hepatic gluconeogenesis; avoid if eGFR < 30). 
 Add-on therapy (based on comorbidities): 
 ASCVD/Heart Failure/CKD: SGLT2 inhibitors (Empagliflozin, Dapagliflozin) or GLP-1RAs 
(Liraglutide, Semaglutide). SGLT2i provide mortality benefit in HF/CKD. 
 High cost/Access issues: Sulfonylureas (Glimepiride - risk of hypoglycemia/weight gain), 
Pioglitazone (risk of edema/HF), DPP-4 inhibitors (Gliptins - weight neutral). 
 Targets: HbAlc < 7.0% for most non-pregnant adults. 
Complications 
 Acute: Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA - mostly Type 1), Hyperosmolar Hyperglycemic State (HHS - 
mostly Type 2), Hypoglycemia. 
 Chronic Microvascular: Retinopathy (Non-proliferative vs. Proliferative), Nephropathy 
(Kimmelstiel-Wilson nodules), Neuropathy (Stocking-glove sensorimotor, Autonomic). 
 Chronic Macrovascular: Coronary Artery Disease (CAD), Peripheral Arterial Disease (PAD), 
Cerebrovascular Disease (Stroke). 
Important Exam Points (NEET-PG) 
 MCQ Pearl: Most common cause of death in DM type 2 is cardiovascular disease (Myocardial 
Infarction). 
 Pathology: Kimmelstiel-Wilson (KW) lesions are nodular glomerulosclerosis, pathognomonic for 
diabetic nephropathy. Stain PAS positive. 
 DKA Triad: Hyperglycemia (> 250 mg/dL), Ketosis, High Anion Gap Metabolic Acidosis (pH 
< 7.3, Bicarb < 18). 
 Treatment of DKA: IV Fluids (most critical initial step - 0.9% NS) - IV Regular Insulin - 
Potassium correction (Insulin drives K ' into cells).

2. HYPERTENSION (HTN) 
Definition 
Sustained elevation of systemic arterial blood pressure. According to ACC/AHA (2017), HTN is defined 
as Systolic BP > 130 mmHg OR Diastolic BP > 80 mmHg. (Note: Many global guidelines, including 
JNC 8 and ESC/ESH, still use > mmHg). 
Etiology 
 Primary (Essential) HTN (90-95%): Idiopathic. Complex interplay of genetics, sympathetic 
nervous system (SNS) hyperactivity, Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS) overactivity, 
defect in natriuresis, high sodium intake, obesity, and alcohol. 
 Secondary HTN (5-10%): 
 Renal: Chronic kidney disease, Renal artery stenosis (fibromuscular dysplasia in young 
females, atherosclerosis in elderly). 
 Endocrine: Primary Hyperaldosteronism (Conn's syndrome), Pheochromocytoma, Cushing's 
syndrome, Acromegaly, Thyroid/Parathyroid disorders. 
o Vascular: Coarctation of the aorta. 
e Drugs: OCPs, NSAIDs, Corticosteroids, Sympathomimetics. 
 Other: Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA). 
Pathophysiology 
Blood Pressure = Cardiac Output (CO) X Systemic Vascular Resistance (SVR). 
 Increased CO (increased fluid volume via excess sodium or RAAS, or increased sympathetic 
drive). 
 Increased SVR (vasoconstriction via Angiotensin II, catecholamines, endothelial 
dysfunction/decreased Nitric Oxide, vascular remodeling/hypertrophy). 
Clinical Features 
 Usually asymptomatic ("Silent Killer"). 
 May present with morning occipital headache, dizziness, tinnitus, or palpitations. 
 Secondary HTN clues: Abdominal bruit (Renal Artery Stenosis), delayed radio-femoral delay 
(Coarctation), episodic sweating/headache/palpitations (Pheochromocytoma), hypokalemia 
(Primary hyperaldosteronism). 
Diagnosis & Investigations 
 Diagnosis: Requires at least two elevated readings on two separate occasions using proper 
technique. Ambulatory BP Monitoring (ABPM) is the gold standard to rule out "White Coat" and 
"Masked" HTN.
o ECG (Left ventricular hypertrophy). 
 Urinanalysis (proteinuria), Serum Creatinine, BUN, eGFR, Electrolytes (Na*, K+, Ca®"). 
 Fasting lipid profile, Fasting glucose. 
 Fundoscopy (Keith-Wagener-Barker grading: | - Tortuosity/Silver wiring, Il - AV nicking, Il - 
Flame hemorrhages/Cotton wool spots, IV - Papilledema). 
Treatment 
Lifestyle Modifications: DASH diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension), Sodium 
restriction (< 1.5 g/day), weight loss (most effective, ~1 mmHg drop per 1kg lost), aerobic 
exercise, moderation of alcohol. 
Pharmacotherapy (First-line agents): 
1. Thiazide diuretics: Chlorthalidone, Hydrochlorothiazide. 
2. Calcium Channel Blockers {CCB): Amlodipine, Nifedipine (Dihydropyridines). 
3. ACE Inhibitors / ARBs: Enalapril, Ramipril / Losartan, Telmisartan. (Compelling indication in 
DM, CKD, HF). 
Note: Black patients respond better to Thiazides or CCBs. Do not combine ACEi and ARB. 
Complications 
Heart: LVH, Heart Failure (HFrEF and HFpEF), CAD, Atrial Fibrillation. 
Brain: Ischemic stroke, Intracerebral hemorrhage (Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysms), Vascular 
dementia. 
Kidney: Hypertensive nephrosclerosis (benign and malignant). 
Vessels: Aortic dissection, Peripheral arterial disease. 
Important Exam Points (NEET-PG) 
Hypertensive Emergency: BP > 180,120 WITH acute target organ damage (Encephalopathy, 
acute MI, pulmonary edema, aortic dissection). Treat with IV medications (Nitroprusside, 
Labetalol, Nicardipine). Goal: Reduce MAP by max 25% in the first hour. 
Hypertensive Urgency: BP > WITHOUT target organ damage. Treat with oral meds. 
Contraindications: ACEi/ARBs are strictly contraindicated in bilateral renal artery stenosis and 
pregnancy. 
Aortic Dissection BP Control: IV Beta-blocker (Esmolol, Labetalol) is first line to reduce d P /dt 
(shear stress), followed by a vasodilator. 
3. BRONCHIAL ASTHMA 
Definition
respiratory symptoms (wheeze, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and cough) that vary over time 
and in intensity, together with variable expiratory airflow limitation. 
Etiology 
 Atopic (Extrinsic): Type | Hypersensitivity reaction to environmental allergens (dust mites, 
pollen, pet dander). IgE mediated. Begins in childhood, associated with positive family history 
and other atopic diseases (eczema, allergic rhinitis). 
 Non-Atopic (Intrinsic): Triggered by respiratory infections (especially viral), cold air, exercise, 
stress, irritating fumes. Normal IgE levels. 
 Drug-Induced: Aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (AERD) - Samter's Triad (Asthma, Aspirin 
sensitivity, Nasal polyps). Due to inhibition of COX, leading to overproduction of leukotrienes. 
 Occupational: Exposure to sensitizing agents at work (e.g., isocyanates, wood dust). 
Pathophysiology 
Exposure to trigger - Activation of Th2 cells - Release of cytokines (IL-4 for IgE class switching, IL- 
5 for eosinophil recruitment, IL-13 for mucus hypersecretion) - IgE binds to mast cells - Cross- 
linking by allergen - Degranulation (Histamine, Leukotrienes, Prostaglandins). 
o Early Phase (Minutes): Bronchoconstriction, edema, increased mucus. 
 Late Phase (Hours): Infiltration by eosinophils, neutrophils, T-cells causing epithelial damage and 
hyperreactivity. 
 Airway Remodeling (Chronic): Smooth muscle hypertrophy, subepithelial fibrosis, mucus gland 
hyperplasia. 
Clinical Features 
 Episodic breathlessness, wheezing, cough (often nocturnal or early morning), chest tightness. 
 Physical Exam: Prolonged expiratory phase, polyphonic widespread expiratory wheeze, 
tachypnea, tachycardia. Pulsus paradoxus in severe attacks. 
Diagnosis & Investigations 
 Pulmonary Function Tests (Spirometry): Obstructive pattern. FEV; /FV C' < 0.7 (or lower 
limit of normal). 
 Reversibility Testing: Increase in FE'V; by > 12% AND > 200 mL after administering a short- 
acting bronchodilator (SABA like Salbutamol). 
 Peak Expiratory Flow Rate (PEFR): Diurnal variability > 10% is diagnostic. 
 Bronchoprovocation Test: Methacholine challenge (if spirometry is normal but asthma is 
suspected). PCy (provocative concentration causing 20% drop in FEV]) < 8 mg/mLis 
positive. 
 Sputum: Curschmann spirals (whorled mucus plugs) and Charcot-Leyden crystals (breakdown 
products of eosinophils - galectin-10).
Relievers: Low-dose Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) + Formoterol (a LABA with fast onset). This is 
the preferred reliever in modern guidelines (MART therapy). Alternatively, SABA (Salbutamol) as 
needed. 
Controllers (Stepwise): 
o Step : As-needed low-dose ICS-Formoterol. 
 Step 3: Low-dose maintenance ICS-LABA. 
 Step 4: Medium-dose maintenance ICS-LABA. 
 Step 5: High-dose ICS-LABA + add-on (LAMA e.g., Tiotropium) or Biologics (Omalizumab 
[anti-IgE], Mepolizumab [anti-IL5]). 
Acute Exacerbation (Status Asthmaticus): Oxygen, inhaled high-dose SABA + Ipratropium, 
systemic corticosteroids (Oral Prednisolone or IV Hydrocortisone). IV Magnesium Sulfate in 
severe cases. 
Complications 
Acute severe asthma (Status Asthmaticus), respiratory failure, pneumothorax, cor pulmonale 
(rare compared to COPD). 
Important Exam Points (NEET-PG) 
Normal or high PaC'O, in an acute asthma attack: Indicates impending respiratory failure 
(muscle fatigue). The patient is usually hypocapnic (respiratory alkalosis) due to tachypnea. 
Omalizumab: Monoclonal antibody against Fc portion of IgE. Used in severe allergic asthma. 
Mepolizumab/Reslizumab: IL-5 antagonists, used in severe eosinophilic asthma. 
Montelukast/Zafirlukast: Leukotriene receptor antagonists. Excellent for Aspirin-induced and 
exercise-induced asthma. 
4. CHRONIC OBSTRUCTIVE PULMONARY DISEASE (COPD) 
Definition 
A common, preventable, and treatable disease characterized by persistent respiratory symptoms and 
irreversible (or poorly reversible) airflow limitation that is due to airway and/or alveolar 
abnormalities usually caused by significant exposure to noxious particles or gases. 
Etiology 
 
Tobacco Smoking: Most common cause (> 90% in developed countries). 
Biomass Fuel Exposure: Major cause in developing countries, especially among women cooking 
indoors. 
Occupational Dusts/Chemicals: Coal miners, silica. 
Genetic: Alpha-1 Antitrypsin (c1-AT) deficiency (causes panacinar emphysema, often lower 
lobes, in young non-smokers or early in smokers).
Two main components often coexist: 
1. Chronic Bronchitis {Airway Disease): Clinically defined as a productive cough for > 3 
months/year for 2 consecutive years. Hypertrophy and hyperplasia of mucus-secreting glands in 
bronchi. Reid Index (thickness of mucosal gland layer / thickness of wall between epithelium and 
cartilage) > 0.4. 
2. Emphysema {Alveolar Disease): Pathologically defined as permanent enlargement of air spaces 
distal to terminal bronchioles with destruction of alveolar walls, without obvious fibrosis. 
Neutrophils/macrophages release elastases; deficiency of anti-elastases (cv;-AT) leads to 
destruction of elastic tissue. Decreased radial traction causes airway collapse during expiration. 
Centriacinar (smoking, upper lobes), Panacinar (cv1-AT deficiency, lower lobes). 
Clinical Features 
 "Pink Puffers" (Emphysema-predominant): Dyspnea, barrel chest, pursed-lip breathing, thin, 
hyperventilating, cachectic. Minimal cough. 
 "Blue Bloaters" (Chronic Bronchitis-predominant): Productive cough, cyanosis, fluid retention 
(cor pulmonale), obese. 
 Signs: Barrel chest (increased AP diameter), hyperresonant to percussion, decreased breath 
sounds, prolonged expiration, wheezes, Hoover's sign (paradoxical indrawing of lower ribs on 
inspiration). 
Diagnosis & Investigations 
 Spirometry: Required for diagnosis. Post-bronchodilator FEV; /FV C < 0.70 confirms 
persistent airflow limitation. Severity staging (GOLD 1-4) based on FEV] % predicted. 
 Arterial Blood Gas (ABG): Hypoxemia, hypercapnia (respiratory acidosis) in advanced disease. 
 CXR: Hyperinflation, flattened diaphragm, increased retrosternal airspace, bullae, narrowed 
cardiac silhouette (emphysema). 
 ECG/Echo: P-pulmonale (tall P waves in II, Ill, aVF), RV hypertrophy, Cor Pulmonale. 
Treatment (GOLD Guidelines) 
 Smoking cessation: The most effective intervention to slow the decline of lung function. 
 Vaccinations: Influenza, Pneumococcal, COVID-19. 
 Pharmacotherapy (grouped by symptoms/exacerbation risk - ABCD assessment): 
 Group A: SABA or SAMA (Ipratropium) as needed. 
 Group B: LABA or LAMA (Tiotropium) daily. 
o Group E (High exacerbation risk): LAMA + LABA, or LAMA + LABA + ICS. (Note: ICS is NEVER 
used as monotherapy in COPD). 
 Phosphodiesterase-4 inhibitors (Roflumilast): For severe chronic bronchitis with recurrent 
exacerbations. 
 Long-Term Oxygen Therapy (LTOT): Prolongs survival in hypoxemic patients.
OR PaO, between 56-59 mmHg with evidence of cor pulmonale, right heart failure, or 
polycythemia (Hematocrit > 55%). Must be worn > 15 hours/day. 
Complications 
 Cor pulmonale (Right heart failure secondary to pulmonary hypertension from chronic hypoxic 
vasoconstriction). 
 Secondary polycythemia. 
 Pneumothorax (rupture of emphysematous bullae). 
 Lung cancer (independent risk factor). 
Important Exam Points (NEET-PG) 
 Reid Index: Pathognomonic for Chronic Bronchitis. Normal < 0.4. 
 Survival Benefit: Only three things have been proven to decrease mortality in COPD: Smoking 
cessation, LTOT, and Lung Volume Reduction Surgery. (Drugs do NOT improve survival, they only 
improve symptoms/exacerbations). 
 Oxygen Therapy in Acute Exacerbation: Give controlled O (Venturi mask targeting 
88 - 92%). Giving high O can abolish the hypoxic drive, worsen V/Q mismatch (Haldane 
effect), and cause fatal hypercapnia. 
5. HEART FAILURE (HF) 
Definition 
A complex clinical syndrome that results from any structural or functional impairment of ventricular 
filling or ejection of blood. It is defined by cardinal symptoms (dyspnea, fatigue) and signs (edema, 
rales) due to elevated intracardiac pressures and/or inadequate cardiac output at rest or during 
exercise. 
Etiology 
 Ischemic Heart Disease/CAD {Most common): Myocardial infarction causing scar/loss of 
functioning myocardium. 
 Hypertension: Causes LV hypertrophy and eventual dysfunction. 
 Valvular Heart Disease: Aortic stenosis/regurgitation, Mitral regurgitation. 
 Cardiomyopathies: Dilated (alcohol, viral, doxorubicin, peripartum), Hypertrophic, Restrictive 
(Amyloidosis, Hemochromatosis). 
 High-Output HF: Thyrotoxicosis, severe anemia, AV fistulas, Beriberi, Paget's disease. 
Pathophysiology 
 Index event decreases Cardiac Output (CO). 
 Neurohormonal Activation (Compensatory mechanisms that become maladaptive):
- increases afterload and myocardial oxygen demand -> toxic to myocytes. 
o RAAS Activation: Decreased renal perfusion - Renin release - Angiotensin Il (potent 
vasoconstrictor, causes remodeling/fibrosis) - Aldosterone (sodium and water retention, 
myocardial fibrosis). 
 Natriuretic Peptides (ANP/BNP): Released in response to atrial/ventricular stretch. Promote 
vasodilation and natriuresis (counter-regulatory). 
Classification 
 HFrEF (Heart Failure with reduced Ejection Fraction): LV EF' < 40%. Systolic dysfunction 
(inability to pump). 
 HFmrEF (Mildly reduced EF): LVEF 41 - 49%. 
 HFpEF (Heart Failure with preserved Ejection Fraction): LV EF > 50%. Diastolic 
dysfunction (inability to relax/fill). Common in elderly females with HTN and LVH. 
Clinical Features 
 Left-Sided HF: Dyspnea on exertion, Orthopnea (dyspnea when supine), Paroxysmal Nocturnal 
Dyspnea (PND), fatigue. Signs: Basal pulmonary crackles/rales, 53 gallop (volume overload), 
laterally displaced apex beat. 
 Right-Sided HF {Most commonly caused by Left-sided HF): Peripheral pitting edema, ascites, 
right upper quadrant pain (hepatic congestion). Signs: Elevated Jugular Venous Pressure (JVP), 
hepatojugular reflux, tender hepatomegaly. 
Diagnosis & Investigations 
 Echocardiography (Gold Standard): Assesses LVEF, chamber sizes, wall thickness, valvular 
function, and diastolic function. 
 BNP or NT-proBNP: Highly sensitive. Excellent for ruling out HF in acute dyspnea (normal levels 
make HF very unlikely). 
 CXR: Cardiomegaly (Cardiothoracic ratio > 0.5), Alveolar edema (Bat's wing appearance), 
Kerley B lines (interstitial edema), Pleural effusion (usually right-sided or bilateral), prominent 
upper lobe vessels (cephalization). 
 ECG: Evidence of LVH, prior MI (Q waves), arrhythmias (Atrial Fibrillation). 
Treatment (HFrEF) 
 The "Fantastic Four" (Provide mortality benefit - Foundational therapy): 
1. ARNI (Sacubitril/Valsartan) or ACEiI/ARB: ARNI is superior. 
2. Beta-Blockers: Bisoprolol, Carvedilol, Metoprolol succinate. (Start low, go slow. Do NOT start 
in acute decompensated state). 
3. Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonists (MRA): Spironolactone, Eplerenone (monitor for 
hyperkalemia).
Symptom Relief: Loop diuretics (Furosemide, Torsemide). Reduce preload/congestion. (No 
mortality benefit). 
 Devices: ICD (Implantable Cardioverter-Defibrillator) for EF < 35% to prevent sudden cardiac 
death. CRT (Cardiac Resynchronization Therapy / Biventricular pacing) for EF < 35% with LBBB 
and QRS > 150 ms. 
Complications 
 Arrhythmias (Atrial fibrillation, Ventricular tachycardia/fibrillation leading to Sudden Cardiac 
Death). 
 Cardiorenal syndrome. 
 Hepatic congestion (Cardiac cirrhosis). 
Important Exam Points (NEET-PG) 
 Drugs that Decrease Mortality in HFrEF: ACEiI/ARBs, ARNI, Beta-blockers, Spironolactone, 
SGLT2i, Hydralazine+Nitrates (in African descent). 
 Drugs that DO NOT decrease mortality: Loop diuretics, Digoxin. 
 Digoxin: Positive inotrope, negative chronotrope. Used for symptom control and in HF with Atrial 
Fibrillation. Toxicity worsened by hypokalemia. Signs of toxicity: Nausea, vomiting, blurry yellow 
vision (xanthopsia), arrhythmias (PVCs most common, Bidirectional VT is characteristic). 
 Heart Failure Cells: Hemosiderin-laden macrophages in the lungs. 
6. MYOCARDIAL INFARCTION (MI/ ACS) 
Definition 
Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) encompasses a spectrum of conditions compatible with acute 
myocardial ischemia and/or infarction, due to an abrupt reduction in coronary blood flow. It includes 
Unstable Angina (UA), Non-ST-Segment Elevation MI (NSTEMI), and ST-Segment Elevation Ml (STEMI). 
MI Definition: Detection of a rise and/or fall of cardiac biomarkers (Troponin) with at least one value 
above the 99th percentile URL, along with evidence of myocardial ischemia (symptoms, ECG 
changes, or imaging). 
Etiology & Pathophysiology 
 Atherosclerosis: Rupture, fissuring, or erosion of an unstable atherosclerotic plague - 
exposure of subendothelial collagen and tissue factor - platelet adhesion and aggregation - 
activation of coagulation cascade - Thrombus formation. 
 Subtotal occlusion: UA or NSTEMI. 
 Total occlusion: STEMI. 
 Type 2 MI: Ischemia due to increased demand or decreased supply in the absence of acute 
plaque rupture (e.g., severe anemia, sepsis, coronary spasm / Prinzmetal angina).
Chest Pain: Retrosternal, crushing/squeezing heavy pain. Radiates to the left arm, neck, jaw, or 
back. Usually lasts > 20 minutes. Not relieved by rest or nitroglycerin. 
 Autonomic symptoms: Profuse diaphoresis (sweating), nausea, vomiting, apprehension (feeling 
of impending doom). 
 Atypical Presentation: Elderly, women, and diabetics may present without chest pain (silent MI), 
primarily with dyspnea, fatigue, or syncope. 
Diagnosis & Investigations 
 12-Lead ECG (Perform within 10 minutes): 
o STEMI: ST-segment elevation > 1 mmin > 2 contiguous leads (except V2-V3, which have 
age/sex specific cutoffs: > 2 mminmen > 40, > 2.5 mminmen < 40, > 1.5 mmin 
women). Followed by T wave inversion and pathological Q waves. New LBBB is considered 
equivalent to STEMI in the right clinical context. 
 Localizing the infarct: 
 Anterior: V1-V4 (LAD territory). 
 Lateral: |, aVvL, V5-V6 (LCx territory). 
 Inferior: I, Ill, aVF (RCA territory, sometimes LCx). 
o NSTEMI/UA: ST depression or T wave inversion. 
 Cardiac Biomarkers: 
 High-Sensitivity Troponin (I or T): Most sensitive and specific. Rises in 3-4 hours, peaks at 24 
hours, stays elevated for 7-14 days. 
o CK-MB: Rises in 4-6 hours, returns to normal in 48-72 hours. Useful for detecting 
reinfarction. 
 Echocardiography: Regional wall motion abnormalities (RWMA). 
Treatment 
 Immediate (MONA-B is historical; currently focus on anti-ischemic and antithrombotic): 
 Oxygen (only if Sa0y < 90%). 
 Nitrates (Sublingual GTN - avoids in Inferior MI/RV infarct as they are preload dependent). 
o Aspirin (300 mg chewed). 
 P2Y12 Inhibitor: Ticagrelor or Prasugrel (preferred over Clopidogrel). 
 Beta-blocker (Oral, to reduce myocardial work; contraindicated in acute HF, heart block, or 
cocaine use). 
 High-intensity Statin (Atorvastatin 80 mg). 
 Anticoagulation: Heparin (UFH or LMWH). 
 Reperfusion Therapy (Crucial for STEMI):
time < 90 minutes (or < 120 minutes if transferred from a non-PCl center). 
 Thrombolysis/Fibrinolysis (Tenecteplase, Alteplase): Used if PCI is unavailable within 120 
minutes. Goal: Door-to-needle time < 30 minutes. (Contraindicated in active bleeding, 
recent surgery, prior hemorrhagic stroke). 
 NSTEMI/UA Management: Antithrombotics + early invasive strategy (angiography within 24-72 
hours based on risk scoring e.g., GRACE/TIMI score). No thrombolysis in NSTEMI! 
Complications 
 Arrhythmias: Ventricular Fibrillation (most common cause of death in the first 48 hours). 
 Mechanical {3-14 days post-MI): Free wall rupture (cardiac tamponade), Ventricular septal 
rupture (harsh holosystolic murmur), Papillary muscle rupture (acute severe mitral regurgitation - 
common in inferior MI). 
 Inflammatory: 
 Fibrinous Pericarditis: 1-3 days post-MI. 
 Dressler Syndrome: 2-8 weeks post-MI (autoimmune fibrinous pericarditis). 
Important Exam Points (NEET-PG) 
 Inferior MI (Right Ventricular Infarction): Suspect if elevated JVP, clear lungs, hypotension. 
Nitrates and Diuretics are strictly contraindicated. Treatment is IV fluids to maintain preload. 
 Marker for Reinfarction: CK-MB. 
 ECGleads: I, Ill, aVF (Inferior - RCA); V1-V4 (Anterior - LAD); I, aVL, V5, V6 (Lateral - LCx). 
7.PNEUMONIA 
Definition 
An acute infection of the pulmonary parenchyma. Classified as: 
 Community-Acquired Pneumonia {(CAP): Acquired outside of a hospital setting or diagnosed 
within 48 hours of admission. 
 Hospital-Acquired Pneumonia (HAP): Occurs > 48 hours after admission. 
 Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia (VAP): Occurs > 48 hours after endotracheal intubation. 
Etiology 
 Typical CAP: Streptococcus pneumoniae (Most common overall), Haemophilus influenzae, 
Moraxella catarrhalis, Staphylococcus aureus (post-viral/influenza). 
 Atypical CAP: Mycoplasma pneumoniae (young adults, dorms), Chlamydophila pneumoniae, 
Legionella pneumophila (air conditioning, water tanks, associated with hyponatremia and 
diarrhea). 
 HAP/VAP: Enteric Gram-negative bacilli (Klebsiella, E. coli), Pseudomonas aeruginosa, MRSA.
epileptics, stroke patients (typically affects dependent lobes: superior segment of lower lobe or 
posterior segment of upper lobe). 
Viral: Influenza, SARS-CoV-2, RSV. 
Pathophysiology 
Microorganisms reach the lower respiratory tract via microaspiration, inhalation, or hematogenous 
spread. Overwhelms alveolar macrophages - acute inflammatory response (cytokines, neutrophils) 
- exudate fills alveoli (consolidation) - V/Q mismatch and hypoxemia. Stages of Lobar Pneumonia: 
Congestion (day 1-2) - Red Hepatization (day 3-4, exudate with RBCs/fibrin) - Gray Hepatization 
(day 5-7, RBCs disintegrate, neutrophils/fibrin remain) - Resolution (day 8+). 
Clinical Features 
Typical: Abrupt onset of high fever with chills/rigors, productive cough (purulent or rust-colored 
sputum), pleuritic chest pain, dyspnea. 
Atypical: Insidious onset, low-grade fever, dry cough, prominent extrapulmonary symptoms 
(headache, myalgia, fatigue). 
Physical Exam: Signs of consolidation: Tachypnea, dullness to percussion, increased tactile vocal 
fremitus, bronchial breath sounds, late inspiratory crackles (crepitations), egophony (E to A 
change), whispering pectoriloquy. 
Diagnosis & Investigations 
Chest X-Ray (CXR): Confirms the diagnosis. Shows lobar consolidation, patchy infiltrates 
(bronchopneumonia), or interstitial infiltrates (atypical). Air bronchograms are a classic sign of 
alveolar space filling. 
Sputum Gram Stain & Culture: Useful for targeting therapy (must obtain before antibiotics if 
possible, but do not delay treatment). 
Blood Cultures: Positive in ~10% of cases. 
Urinary Antigens: For Legionella and S. pneumoniae. 
Biomarkers: Procalcitonin (elevated in bacterial, low in viral). 
Severity Scoring (CURB-65) 
Helps determine the site of care (Outpatient vs. Inpatient vs. ICU): 
Confusion 
Urea > 7 mmol/L (BUN > 19 mg/dL) 
Respiratory rate > 30/min 
Blood pressure (Systolic < 90 or Diastolic < 60) 
Age > 65 years (Score O-1: Outpatient; Score 2: Ward admission; Score 3+: Severe, consider ICU). 
Treatment (CAP)
Outpatient (Comorbidities): Oral Amoxicillin/Clavulanate + Macrolide, OR Respiratory 
Fluoroquinolone (Levofloxacin/Moxifloxacin). 
 Inpatient (Ward): IV Beta-lactam (Ceftriaxone) + Macrolide (Azithromycin), OR IV Respiratory 
Fluoroquinolone. 
 Inpatient (ICU): IV Beta-lactam (Ceftriaxone, Cefotaxime) + IV Azithromycin or Respiratory 
Fluoroquinolone. (Add Vancomycin/Linezolid if MRSA suspected; use Piperacillin-Tazobactam if 
Pseudomonas suspected). 
Complications 
 Parapneumonic effusion, Empyema (pus in pleural space), Lung abscess (tissue necrosis with air- 
fluid level, common with S. aureus, Klebsiella, Anaerobes), Sepsis, ARDS. 
Important Exam Points (NEET-PG) 
 Klebsiella pneumoniae: "Currant jelly" sputum, bulging fissure sign on CXR, common in 
alcoholics. 
 Legionella: Hyponatremia, diarrhea, relative bradycardia, neurological symptoms. Diagnosis by 
urine antigen. Treatment: Macrolides or Fluoroquinolones. 
 Mycoplasma pneumoniae: Cold agglutinins (IgM leading to autoimmune hemolytic anemia), 
erythema multiforme. 
 Pneumocystis jirovecii (PCP): Fungal pneumonia in HIV/AIDS (C'D4 < 200). Bilateral ground- 
glass opacities. Treat/Prophylax with Cotrimoxazole (TMP-SMX). 
8. TUBERCULOSIS (TB) 
Definition 
A chronic, necrotizing granulomatous infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis 
complex, primarily affecting the lungs (Pulmonary TB) but can affect any organ (Extrapulmonary TB). 
Etiology 
 Mycobacterium tuberculosis is an obligate aerobe, non-motile, non-spore-forming bacillus. 
 Has a lipid-rich cell wall containing mycolic acid, making it acid-fast (retains carbol-fuchsin stain 
despite acid-alcohol wash). 
Pathophysiology 
 Primary TB: Inhalation of droplet nuclei - Bacilli reach alveoli - Phagocytosed by alveolar 
macrophages but survive intracellularly by inhibiting phagolysosome fusion - Multiplication - 
Cell-mediated immunity (Type IV hypersensitivity) develops after 2-4 weeks - C'D4™" T cells 
secrete IFN-y - Macrophage activation and differentiation into epithelioid cells and Langhans 
giant cells - Formation of caseating granulomas. 
 Ghon focus: Subpleural lesion in mid/lower zones.
Ranke complex: Calcified Ghon complex. 
 Secondary (Reactivation) TB: Reactivation of latent infection, usually when host immunity drops 
(HIV, diabetes, steroids, aging). Typically occurs in the apices of the lungs (high oxygen tension). 
Tends to cavitate. 
Clinical Features 
 Pulmonary TB: Chronic cough (dry initially, then purulent), Hemoptysis, Low-grade evening-rise 
fever, Night sweats, Anorexia, Weight loss. 
 Extrapulmonary TB (EPTB): 
 Lymphadenitis: Most common EPTB. Painless, matted cervical nodes (Scrofula). 
 Pleural TB: Exudative effusion with high lymphocytes and high ADA (Adenosine Deaminase). 
 Skeletal TB: Pott's disease (TB of the spine), cold abscesses. 
 CNS TB: TB meningitis (basal exudates, cranial nerve palsies, CSF shows high protein, low 
glucose, lymphocytosis). 
 Genitourinary: "Sterile" pyuria, epididymitis, female infertility (endometrial TB). 
Diagnosis & Investigations 
 Sputum Smear Microscopy: Ziehl-Neelsen (ZN) stain or Fluorescent staining (Auramine- 
Rhodamine). Requires at least 2 samples (one early morning). 
 CBNAAT (GeneXpert MTB/RIF) / Truenat: Nucleic acid amplification test. Gold standard for 
rapid diagnosis. Detects TB DNA and Rifampicin resistance within 2 hours. Endorsed by NTEP as 
the initial diagnostic test. 
 Culture: MGIT (Mycobacteria Growth Indicator Tube) - liquid culture is faster (1-3 weeks) than 
solid Lowenstein-Jensen (LJ) medium (4-8 weeks). Gold standard for drug susceptibility testing. 
 CXR: Apical cavitations, fibronodular infiltrates, miliary mottling (millet seed appearance). 
 Latent TB Tests: Mantoux test (Tuberculin Skin Test - measures delayed hypersensitivity) and 
IGRA (Interferon-Gamma Release Assay - e.g., QuantiFERON-TB Gold). These do not distinguish 
latent from active disease. 
Treatment (NTEP Guidelines) 
 First-Line Drugs (HRZE): 
 Isoniazid {(H): Bactericidal. SE: Peripheral neuropathy (prevent with Pyridoxine/Bé), Hepatitis, 
Drug-induced lupus. 
 Rifampicin (R): Bactericidal. SE: Orange-red urine/secretions, Hepatitis, potent CYP450 
inducer (decreases efficacy of OCPs, ART). 
 Pyrazinamide {Z): Acts in acidic environment (intracellular). SE: Hyperuricemia (gout), most 
hepatotoxic of all. 
 Ethambutol (E): Bacteriostatic. SE: Optic neuritis (decreased visual acuity, red-green color 
blindness). NOT hepatotoxic.
Intensive Phase: 2 months of HRZE. 
 Continuation Phase: 4 months of HRE (some guidelines use HR). Total duration: 6 months. 
Complications 
 Massive hemoptysis (rupture of Rasmussen's aneurysm). 
 Spontaneous pneumothorax, bronchiectasis. 
 Aspergilloma (fungus ball forming in a pre-existing healed TB cavity). Cor Puimonale. 
Important Exam Points (NEET-PG) 
 MDR-TB: Resistant to at least Isoniazid and Rifampicin. 
 XDR-TB: MDR-TB + resistant to any fluoroquinolone and at least one additional Group A drug 
(Bedaquiline or Linezolid). 
 Most Hepatotoxic Anti-TB Drug: Pyrazinamide > Isoniazid > Rifampicin. Ethambutol and 
Streptomycin are safe for the liver. 
 Safein Pregnancy: H, R, Z, E. (Streptomycin is absolutely contraindicated - causes fetal 
ototoxicity). 
9. ANEMIA 
Definition 
A reduction in the total circulating red cell mass, clinically indicated by a decrease in Hemoglobin (Hb) 
concentration or Hematocrit below the lower limit of normal for age, sex, and altitude. (WHO criteria: 
Hb < 13.0 g/dL in adult men, < 12.0 g/dL in non-pregnant adult women, < 11.0 g/dL in pregnancy). 
Classification based on Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV) 
1. Microcytic Hypochromic (MCV < 80 fL): TAILS (Thalassemia, Anemia of chronic disease, Iron 
deficiency, Lead poisoning, Sideroblastic anemia). 
2. Macrocytic (MCV > 100 fL): 
 Megaloblastic: Vitamin B12 deficiency, Folate deficiency. (Impaired DNA synthesis). 
 Non-Megaloblastic: Liver disease, Alcoholism, Hypothyroidism, Reticulocytosis. 
3. Normocytic Normochromic {MCV 80-100 fL): Acute blood loss, Hemolysis, Chronic kidney 
disease (low EPO), Aplastic anemia. 
A. Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA) - Most Common Worldwide 
 Etiology: Chronic blood loss (Gl bleed, heavy menses, hookworm in developing nations), 
increased demand (pregnancy, childhood), decreased absorption (Celiac disease, post- 
gastrectomy). 
 Pathophysiology: Depletion of iron stores (Ferritin) - Lack of iron for heme synthesis -

pencillcigar cells. 
 Iron Profile: Serum Ferritin (Low - most sensitive), Serum Iron (Low), TIBC (High), Transferrin

 Treatment: Identify and treat the underlying cause. Oral elemental iron (Ferrous 
sulfate/fumarate/ascorbate). IV iron if oral is intolerant or malabsorption is present. Reticulocyte 
count increases in 5-7 days; Hb normalizes in 2 months. 
B. Megaloblastic Anemia (B12 / Folate Deficiency) 
 Etiology: 
 Vit B12 (Cobalamin): Pernicious anemia (autoantibodies against intrinsic factor or parietal 
cells), strict vegan diet, Crohn's disease, terminal ileum resection, Diphyllobothrium latum 
(fish tapeworm). 
 Folate (B9): Dietary lack (tea and toast diet), alcoholism, increased demand (pregnancy), 
drugs (Methotrexate, Phenytoin). 
 Pathophysiology: Defective DNA synthesis causing nuclear-cytoplasmic asynchrony. 
 Clinical Features: Pallor, mild jaundice (ineffective erythropoiesis), beefy red tongue. 
 Specific to B12: Subacute Combined Degeneration of Spinal Cord (demyelination of dorsal 
columns and lateral corticospinal tracts) causing loss of position/vibration sense, ataxia, 
upper motor neuron signs. Folate deficiency has NO neurological symptoms. 
 Diagnosis: High MCV. Peripheral smear: Macro-ovalocytes, hypersegmented neutrophils (> 5 
lobes). 
 Homocysteine is elevated in both. 
 Methylmalonic acid (MMA) is elevated ONLY in B12 deficiency. 
 Treatment: Parenteral/oral B12, Oral Folic acid. Caution: Treating B12 deficiency with folate alone 
corrects the anemia but allows neurological damage to progress irreversibly. 
C. Hemolytic Anemias 
 Features: Jaundice (unconjugated bilirubinemia), gallstones (pigment stones), splenomegaly, 
dark urine. Lab: High reticulocyte count, high LDH, low Haptoglobin. 
 Sickle Cell Anemia: Point mutation (Glutamic acid to Valine at 6th position of 5-globin chain). 
Causes HbS polymerization under hypoxia. Presents with vaso-occlusive crises (pain, dactylitis), 
autosplenectomy (risk of encapsulated organism infections). Diagnosis: Hb electrophoresis. 
Treatment: Hydroxyurea (increases HbF). 
 Thalassemia: Quantitative defect in globin chain synthesis. 3-Thalassemia major presents with 
severe anemia at 6 months of age (when HbF transitions to HbA), hepatosplenomegaly, "crew- 
cut" appearance on skull X-ray, chipmunk facies. Diagnosis: Hb electrophoresis (elevated HbA2 
and HbF).

 Pancytopenia with low reticulocyte count: Think Aplastic Anemia (bone marrow hypocellularity,

 Schistocytes (Helmet cells): Microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (TTP, HUS, DIC). 
10. THYROID DISORDERS 
Definition 
Disorders involving abnormal production of thyroid hormones (T3 and T4), leading to a systemic state 
of decreased metabolic rate (Hypothyroidism) or increased metabolic rate 
(Hyperthyroidism/Thyrotoxicosis). 
A. HYPOTHYROIDISM 
 Etiology: 
 Primary (Most common): Hashimoto's thyroiditis (autoimmune), latrogenic (post-radioactive 
iodine or thyroidectomy), lodine deficiency (most common cause worldwide). 
 Secondary/Tertiary: Pituitary adenoma, Hypothalamic disease (rare). 
 Pathophysiology: Hashimoto's involves C D8 T-cell mediated destruction of thyroid follicles 
and autoantibodies. Lack of T3/T4 decreases basal metabolic rate and increases deposition of 
glycosaminoglycans (myxedema). 
 Clinical Features: Fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain despite poor appetite, constipation, dry 
thick skin, hair loss (outer third of eyebrows), bradycardia, delayed relaxation phase of deep 
tendon reflexes, hoarseness, menstrual irregularities (menorrhagia), depressed mood. 
 Diagnosis: 
o Primary: High TSH, Low Free T4. (Subclinical: High TSH, Normal Free T4). 
 Secondary: Low/Normal TSH, Low Free T4. 
 Antibodies: Anti-TPO (Thyroid Peroxidase) and Anti-Thyroglobulin antibodies are massively 
elevated in Hashimoto's. 
 Treatment: Levothyroxine (synthetic T4) once daily on an empty stomach. Monitor TSH levels 
every 6-8 weeks to adjust the dose. Target TSH is typically 0.5-4.0 mIU/L (tighter control in 
pregnancy).

B. HYPERTHYROIDISM (THYROTOXICOSIS) 
 Etiology: 
 Graves' Disease (Most common, 60-80%): Autoimmune disorder. 
 Toxic Multinodular Goiter (TMNG): Autonomous functioning thyroid nodules (usually older 
adults). 
 Toxic Adenoma: Single hyperfunctioning nodule. 
 Thyroiditis: Subacute (De Quervain's - painful, post-viral), Silent/Postpartum. Initial 
destruction releases pre-formed hormone (transient hyperthyroidism). 
 Pathophysiology of Graves": IgG autoantibodies bind to and stimulate the TSH receptor (Thyroid 
Stimulating Immunoglobulins - TSI). Drives excess T3/T4 production and diffuse glandular growth. 
 Clinical Features: Heat intolerance, weight loss despite increased appetite, palpitations, 
tremors, sweating, frequent bowel movements, anxiety, tachycardia, atrial fibrillation. 
 Specific to Graves' (Extrathyroidal manifestations due to TSI action on fibroblasts): 
Exophthalmos/Proptosis (bulging eyes), Pretibial myxedema (non-pitting edema on shins), 
Thyroid acropachy (clubbing). 
 Diagnosis: 
 Low/suppressed TSH (< 0.1), High Free T4 and/or T3. 
 Antibodies: TSI/ TRAB (TSH Receptor Antibodies) positive in Graves'. 
 Radioactive lodine Uptake (RAIU) Scan: 
 Diffuse high uptake: Graves'. 
 Patchy multiple areas of high uptake: TMNG. 
 Single hot nodule: Toxic adenoma. 
 Lowl/absent uptake: Thyroiditis or exogenous hormone intake (factitious).

Hepatotoxicity.
